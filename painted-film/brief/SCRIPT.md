# The Life of a Seed — Karma in 66 seconds

**Format:** 1080 × 1080 square · 66 s · 30 fps · voiceover + tanpura drone · no people's faces, no deities, everything drawn in code.

**Reference:** Kevin Ngo's "The life of a fruit fly" (27 s, 1080²): ~20 rapid beats that cut between two looks: **warm, cross-hatched naturalist illustration** and **dark navy scientific diagram**. Match cuts carry each beat into the next, and the film ends on its opening image, so it plays as a loop.

**Central metaphor:** Sanskrit already supplies it. *Karma* = action, *bīja* = seed, *phala* = fruit. Karma is shown as a seed's life cycle (sown → stored → ripens → fruits → reseeds), and the cycle breaks only when the seed is roasted in the fire of knowledge (the classic Vedāntic "roasted seed" image, Gītā 4.37).

**Registers**
- **WARM** = the lived world: cream paper, pencil hatching, mango orange, terracotta, wood grain, courtyard tiles.
- **DARK** = the inner mechanism: navy ground, thin lavender linework, glowing dots, construction circles, ruler ticks, small Devanagari + romanised specimen labels.

**Word count:** ~150 words of voiceover, for a calm ~140 wpm read with breaths. To make a silent cut like the reference, drop the voiceover. The Devanagari labels and on-screen beats still carry the idea.

**Sound:** a tanpura drone on Sa–Pa. A soft tabla/clay *tok* lands on each hard cut. A shimmering bell plays on the spark (beats 1, 2, 13). A low whoosh follows the arrow in beat 5. The fire crackles in beat 11, then everything drops to near-silence for mokṣa.

**Sanskrit accuracy notes**
- *Karma* (कर्म) literally means action. Its result is *karma-phala* (कर्मफल).
- *Saṃskāra* = latent impression; *vāsanā* = tendency or desire that arises from impressions.
- The three kinds of karma: *sañcita* (accumulated), *prārabdha* (already set in motion, being lived out in this life), and *āgāmi* / *kriyamāṇa* (being created now). The archer analogy is traditional in Vedānta teaching.
- Gītā 2.22 (worn-out clothes), 2.47 (your right is to action, never to its fruits), 4.37 (the fire of knowledge reduces all karma to ashes).
- The roasted seed (*bhṛṣṭa-bīja*) image is a standard Advaita illustration of how a liberated person's actions no longer bind.
- Traditions differ. Some, for example, hold that prārabdha still plays out after liberation, the way a potter's wheel keeps spinning. The script stays with broadly shared ideas.
