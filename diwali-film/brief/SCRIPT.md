# Diwali: "A Row of Lamps"

1080×1080 · 69.5 s · 30 fps · Company School painted watercolour · Sarvam `bulbul:v4-flash` / `shubh_en_narration` · synthesized tanpura + bells. No faces, no deities: Rama and Lakshmi are named in the narration and shown only by symbols (footprints, sandals, boat, lotus). Everything is drawn in code.

Beat times are set from the measured voiceover (`voiceover/assemble.py`), not the other way round.

| # | Time | Picture | Narration |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0:00–5.6 | New-moon dusk wash, one clay diya on a ground wash lights up. Title "Diwali". | Once a year, on the darkest night of autumn, a whole country lights up. |
| 2 | 5.6–10.7 | Five diyas lit one by one. **दीपावली · Dīpāvali · a row of lamps** | Diwali, Dīpāvali, means a row of lamps. |
| 3 | 10.7–17.9 | City gate, forest, path; sandals left at the gate; footprints walk into the trees. | The oldest tale begins in Ayodhya. A prince, Rama, is sent to the forest for fourteen years. |
| 4 | 17.9–24.7 | 14 ink tally marks; monsoon cloud and rain; bare winter branch; the road home to a lit gate. | Fourteen monsoons. Fourteen winters. Then, at last, the road home. |
| 5 | 24.7–36.0 | Night city silhouette, only a pencil sliver of moon. Windows and rooftops fill with lamps; a lantern boat on the river. | But the night he returns is the new moon, amāvasya, and no one can see the way. So Ayodhya lights lamps, in every window, on every roof. |
| 6 | 36.0–43.0 | Close-up diya with pencil callouts: clay, oil, wick. The flame lights last. | A diya is the simplest thing. Clay, oil, a cotton wick. Yet it is enough. |
| 7 | 43.0–47.8 | Rangoli drawn dot by dot, outlined, then washed in pink, teal, vermilion; a diya each side. | At each doorstep, a rangoli is drawn, a welcome in colour. |
| 8 | 47.8–56.6 | Five days as five symbols over five diyas (broom, coin pot, lotus, sweets, thread), then a bright doorway with a lotus and red footprints stepping in. | Over five days, families clean, feast, and give. Lakshmi, they say, visits the brightest home. |
| 9 | 56.6–61.9 | Four regional tellings (lantern, lotus, boat, quiver), each with its own lamp, merge into one flame on the hero diya. | Every region tells it a little differently. The heart is the same. |
| 10 | 61.9–69.5 | The hero diya lights others by a travelling spark; a field of flames; all fade back to the single opening diya. **शुभ दीपावली**. | A flame loses nothing by lighting another. So light one, and pass it on. |

Changes from the approved table: beat 9 uses a hanging lantern instead of a mustard-oil lamp; beat 4 ends on a lit city gate at the end of a road.
