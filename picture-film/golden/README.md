# Golden set: what "good" looks like here, and what was turned down

Open these three images before judging any new film. Put your own `qa/paintings.jpg` next to `accepted.jpg` and ask which sheet a stranger would say came from the better film. If the answer is not "can't tell", find out why.

- `accepted.jpg`: the ten paintings of the locked Dussehra film (GPT Image 2.6, high, 2K).
- `accepted-film.jpg`: the same film as stills, with title, captions and camera positions.
- `rejected.jpg`: eight frames that were made for the same film and turned down.

## Why the accepted ones work
| | What to notice |
|---|---|
| A1 | The subject sits in the middle band. The top quarter is bare paper for the title and the bottom is bare for captions. Two figures, one action, read in a second. |
| A2 | One idea made visible: the row of moons *is* the "twenty days". The scholar gives the abstract line a person to look at. |
| A3, A5 | Fire is the brightest thing on the sheet and the figures face it. The eye lands where the narration is. |
| A4 | The richest frame, yet still airy: the arches frame the action, and the pouring water is at the centre. |
| A6 | A lot of sky. Empty space is what makes it feel like flight. |
| A7 | The only full-sheet image (a map). It earns the exception because the camera travels across it. |
| A8 | Movement left to right, so the pan has something to follow. |
| A9 | Gesture carries the meaning: one man kneels, the other bends to lift him. |
| A10 | Night as a vignette with soft edges on the same cream paper, not a dark full-bleed frame. |

Across all ten: the same cream paper, the same brown pen line, the same six or seven colours, faces calm and small, edges fading into paper, nothing written inside the picture.

## Why the rejected ones were turned down
| | Reason |
|---|---|
| R1 | Nano Banana. The sheet is drawn as an object on a mount, so a fifth of the frame is border. Flat, doll-like faces and proportions. (The prompt asked for a "deckle edge on a darker mount"; that phrase was removed afterwards.) |
| R2 | FLUX 3. The king came out near-black and reads as a demon, the opposite of the story. Crowded, and it is unclear who is doing what. Devotional-print gloss. |
| R3 | Nano Banana. Thin and empty: clip-art outline, no depth, small stiff figures. Restraint tipped over into blandness. |
| R4 | FLUX 3. Grey-brown and sooty. The mood is grim where the script is joyful. Colour is part of the meaning. |
| R5-R8 | The code-drawn version of the same beats: symbols only (bow, crown, lotus, tally marks). Accurate, tidy, and the user's verdict was "too basic". Nobody to look at, so nothing to feel. R7 is also the wedge-shaped map: India drawn as a plain triangle. |

The pattern: frames fail by being **empty** (R3, R5-R8), **heavy** (R2, R4), or **framed wrong** (R1). The accepted frames are detailed in the middle and quiet at the edges.

## Also turned down, not pictured
- A score synthesized in code (tanpura, bells). Verdict by ear: "the audio isn't good". Scores are generated (see `brief/PROMPTS.md`).
- Sound effects (whooshes, bells) under the paintings. Removed; voice and score only.
- A faint pencil "ghost" copy under each caption. A reviewer found it confusing.
- Voice lines squeezed with `atempo` 1.3-1.4x. They sounded rushed. Tempo stays at 1.0.
