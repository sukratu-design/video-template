## Changes from the draft
1. "Makar Sankranti always falls on January 14" -> "stays near January 14". It falls on 14 or 15 January and drifts later over centuries.
2. "On this day the sun ... begins its northward journey, Uttarayana" -> "Tradition calls it Uttarayana, the turn north." Astronomically the northward turn starts at the December solstice.
3. "It is the winter solstice." -> removed. False: the solstice is 21/22 December, about 23-24 days earlier; the two coincided around 291 CE.
4. "the sun enters Capricorn" kept as "the sun enters Makara, Capricorn", with the Indian name first (sidereal sign).
5. "In the Mahabharata, Bhishma lay on his bed of arrows and waited 58 days for this day to die." -> "Bhishma waited for it on his bed of arrows." Dropped "58 days" (the verse says 58 nights; other counts exist; not needed for the answer) and "to die" (sensitive and unnecessary). "In the Mahabharata" cut for length.
6. "So across India people fly kites to welcome the longer days." -> "Kites fly in Gujarat." Kite flying is regional, not pan-Indian; and days have been lengthening since the solstice, not since Sankranti.
7. Added the answer line "Its date holds because it follows the sun." (TASTE: last line answers the title plainly.)
